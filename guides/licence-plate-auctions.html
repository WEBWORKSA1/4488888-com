Licence plate auctions from Dubai to London

Lucky Numbers Lab · 8 min read · Updated October 2026

The record: P 7

In April 2023, a Dubai plate bearing just the letter P and the number 7 sold for 55 million dirhams — about US$15 million — at a charity auction, making it the most expensive licence plate on record. Proceeds went to a food-aid endowment. It beat the previous record, the single-digit plate "1", which sold in Abu Dhabi in 2008 for 52.2 million dirhams.

Why would anyone pay that? Buyers in the Gulf describe plates as public status markers: a single digit on a car is instantly legible as rank and wealth. Charity auctions add a reputational reason to bid high.

Hong Kong: luck meets scarcity

Hong Kong's Transport Department runs regular auctions of special registration marks. Single letters lead — "W" reached HK$26 million in 2021, and "R" HK$25.5 million in 2023 at a Lunar New Year sale — but lucky numbers compete too. "18" (will prosper) and "28" (easy prosperity, in Cantonese) each sold for around US$2 million. Marks containing 4 are discounted.

The UK: a mature dealer market

In the UK, the DVLA sells personalised registrations through timed online auctions and direct sales, and a large licensed dealer market handles resales. Valuations focus on whether a plate spells a name or word, how few characters it has, and its age-related format (older formats can be put on newer cars, not the reverse). Dealers routinely offer free valuations — the same lead-generation pattern we use.

Australia and elsewhere

Australian states auction historic numeric plates. In New South Wales a "4" plate reportedly sold for about A$1.6 million in 2017 — scarcity outweighing tetraphobia. In the US, rules vary by state and prices rarely approach these levels, although a low-number Delaware plate has exceeded US$400,000.

How auctions work (generally)

  1. Register as a bidder with the official authority or licensed auction house; some require a deposit.
  2. Check eligibility — residency, vehicle ownership and format rules differ by jurisdiction.
  3. Set a ceiling. Add buyer's premiums, transfer fees and taxes to your maximum bid.
  4. Bid live or absentee. Many sales now run online with countdown timers.
  5. Complete the transfer through the official process before using the plate.
Scam warning: authorities contact winners only through published channels. Be wary of messages asking for "reservation fees" or payment outside official systems.
