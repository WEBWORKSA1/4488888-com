Numeric domain names: the complete investor's guide

Lucky Numbers Lab · 9 min read · Updated October 2026

What counts as a numeric domain

A numeric domain is a name made entirely of digits — 88.com, 360.com, 4488888.com. They are a distinct corner of the domain market because their value is driven less by English meaning and more by scarcity, pattern and, above all, the preferences of Chinese-speaking buyers, who brokers estimate account for the large majority of demand.

A common rule of thumb in the trade: if a numeric domain is still available to register at standard price, it is almost certainly not valuable.

The "Chinese premium" rules

  • 8 is king, followed by 6 and 9.
  • 4 is a defect. A single 4 can cut value by around half in the Chinese aftermarket.
  • Zero is acceptable, but a leading 0 is a mild negative.
  • Meaningful phrases (168, 518, 520, 1314) and brand-like readings add value.
  • Patterns — AABB, ABAB, AAAA, ABCABC, ascending runs — add value at every length.

Liquidity and market cycles

Numeric names had an extraordinary run in 2015–2016, when Chinese investors poured into short .com domains and prices for low-quality short names jumped several-fold. A correction followed in 2016–2017. Today the market is more selective: premium patterns still sell, but mid-tier names can take years to find a buyer. Treat numerics as a long-hold, illiquid asset unless the pattern is exceptional.

Where trades happen

Chinese-language numeric auction platforms handle much of the volume, alongside global aftermarkets with escrow and a small number of specialist brokers. Price discovery often happens in Chinese-language markets — Western owners who only list on English-language platforms can under-price their names.

A simple pricing method

  1. Classify the tier by length (the single biggest factor).
  2. Score the digits for 8/6/9 density and the presence and position of 4. Our analyzer does this instantly.
  3. Find comparables — reported sales of the same length and similar pattern in the last 24 months.
  4. Adjust for extension: .com leads; .cn and .net follow at a steep discount.
  5. Decide your market: list where the culturally motivated buyers are.
  6. Always use escrow and never transfer before funds are secured.

Case study: a 7-digit name like 4488888.com

Seven-digit numerics are a large pool, so value comes almost entirely from pattern. 4488888 has two distinct digits and a five-digit run of eights — outstanding memorability — but leads with a double 4. For a Chinese end-user that prefix is a real objection; for Western, Indian or Gulf buyers it reads as a clean, repeating, memorable string. That split is exactly why the right audience matters more than the number.
