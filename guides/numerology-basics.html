Numerology basics: Pythagorean, Chaldean & Vedic

Lucky Numbers Lab · 9 min read · Updated October 2026

Numerology is the practice of assigning symbolic meaning to numbers derived from names and dates. It is a cultural and spiritual tradition, not a science — but it is widely used in naming businesses and babies, choosing dates and picking numbers, so it is worth understanding properly.

The core move: reduction

Almost every system reduces a long number to a single digit by adding its digits repeatedly. 1987 → 1+9+8+7 = 25 → 2+5 = 7. Many Western practitioners stop at 11, 22 or 33, called master numbers, rather than reducing them further.

Pythagorean (Western) numerology

The most common system in Europe and North America maps letters to 1–9 in alphabetical order: A=1, B=2 … I=9, then J=1 again.

Worked example: Life Path

For a birth date of 14 August 1990: month 8 → 8; day 14 → 5; year 1990 → 19 → 10 → 1. Then 8+5+1 = 14 → 5. Life Path 5: freedom, change, adventure. Reducing each part first is the standard method because it preserves master numbers that a single big sum can hide.

Core numbers

  • Life Path — from the birth date; your broad path.
  • Expression — from all letters of the full name.
  • Soul Urge — from the vowels.
  • Personality — from the consonants.
  • Personal Year — birth month + birth day + current year.

Chaldean numerology

Older and based on sound rather than alphabet order, Chaldean numerology assigns only the values 1–8 to letters. Nine is considered sacred and is not assigned to any letter, though it can still appear in totals. Practitioners also read the unreduced compound number (for example 23) before reducing. Because the letter values differ, Chaldean and Pythagorean results for the same name often disagree — something to keep in mind when testing brand names.

Vedic (Indian) numerology

Indian numerology links each root number to a celestial ruler: 1 Sun, 2 Moon, 3 Jupiter, 4 Rahu, 5 Mercury, 6 Venus, 7 Ketu, 8 Saturn, 9 Mars. It distinguishes the psychic number (from the birth day) from the destiny number (from the full birth date), and is commonly consulted for name spellings, business names, vehicle numbers and house numbers. Notably, 8 here is Saturn's number — serious and karmic — quite unlike its "wealth" reputation in Chinese culture.

Lo Shu grid

A related Chinese tool, the Lo Shu magic square, places the digits of a birth date on a 3×3 grid where every row, column and diagonal sums to 15. Practitioners read the "missing numbers" as areas to develop. It is popular in Indian numerology circles too.

Using numerology sensibly

  • Use it as a creative constraint and a cultural signal, not a decision-maker.
  • If your customers care about it, it matters commercially — regardless of whether you believe it.
  • Never let it override safety, health, legal or financial judgement.
