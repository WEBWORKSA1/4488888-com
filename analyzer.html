How the score works

The analyzer models the cultural and psychological preferences that measurably move prices in premium-number markets. It is built from four separate readings, because a number that delights a buyer in Shenzhen may leave a buyer in Tokyo or Toronto cold.

1. Chinese prosperity (38%)

Each digit is weighted by its Mandarin and Cantonese sound associations: 8 (bā ≈ fā, prosper) is strongest, followed by 6 (smooth) and 9 (long-lasting). 4 (sì ≈ sǐ, death) is penalised. Known phrases add or subtract points — 168 ("prosper all the way"), 518 ("I will prosper"), 1314 ("a lifetime"), and the avoided 14, 24 and 74. The final digit gets extra weight because buyers read endings first.

2. Pattern and recall (34%)

Fewer unique digits, long runs of the same digit, ascending or descending sequences, palindromes and mirrored halves all make a number easier to remember — which is why repeating-digit phone numbers command premiums with advertisers regardless of culture.

3. Numerology root (16%)

We reduce the digits to a single root (keeping the master numbers 11, 22 and 33) and read it in Pythagorean and Vedic terms. Root 8 aligns with material success in Western numerology but with Saturn's slow discipline in Vedic numerology — a good reminder that traditions disagree.

4. Japanese reading (12%)

In Japanese, 4 (shi) and 9 (ku, suffering) are avoided, while 7 and 8 (whose character widens downward, suggesting growth) are favoured. The combination 49 is particularly unwelcome.
